About GERI

The Global Ecosystem Research Infrastructure (GERI) is an integrated, international network of site-based research infrastructures working to advance understanding of ecosystem function and change across global biomes. By providing harmonized ecological data from long-term observation sites, GERI supports high-impact scientific research and enables informed decision-making to address pressing environmental and societal challenges.

GERI fosters collaboration among its six-founding continental-scale partners, facilitating new insights into global ecological processes that span regions, disciplines, and decades. Through open data practices, standardized methodologies, and a shared commitment to interoperability, GERI enables the scientific and education communities with comprehensive data to respond more effectively to climate change, biodiversity loss, and ecological hazards.

Founding GERI Partners

The managing institutions that operate six different continental-scale ecosystem research infrastructures (RIs) signed a landmark Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on December 8, 2020. The founding RIs — coming from Africa, Asia, Australia, Europe (two RIs) and North America — have a true global coverage. They are expressly committed to developing the first-ever Global Ecosystem Research Infrastructure (GERI).

Data Harmonization: NSF – AccelNet Pilot Project

Addressing global environmental challenges such as drought and climate change requires environmental analyses at a global scale, yet data from different sources remain fragmented and decentralized. While individual Research Infrastructures (RIs) effectively monitor ecosystems at national and continental scales, global environmental research requires more collaboration to bring these data together.

Each research infrastructure produces high-value observations using its own data models, terminology, units, temporal conventions, quality-control systems, and metadata practices. These differences can make it difficult to combine observations across infrastructures.

GERI developed a scalable data harmonization workflow that deals with a variety of data products from multiple research infrastructures and geographic locations, standardizes variable names, units, timestamps, site identifiers, and dataset structures while preserving the provenance and identity of the contributing source data. The resulting products support discovery, comparison, reuse, and cross-infrastructure analysis in accordance with FAIR data principles.

AccelNet-Implementation: Global Ecosystem Research Infrastructure (GERI): Harmonizing Data to address Ecological Drought

GERI Harmonized Datasets on DataONE

This portal provides access to the harmonized data products developed through the GERI Accelnet ecological drought pilot. The current release integrates observations from NEON, TERN, ICOS, eLTER, SAEON and EFTEON.

Five products generated through the core GERI cross-infrastructure harmonization pipeline are available:

  • Air temperature: Mean air temperature harmonized to a common schema at 30-minute resolution.
  • Precipitation: Bulk precipitation harmonized to a common schema and aggregated to 60-minute intervals.
  • Soil Moisture: Mean volumetric soil water content expressed as a percentage and harmonized at 30-minute resolution.
  • Soil Temperature: Mean soil temperature harmonized to a common schema at 30-minute resolution.
  • Soil Texture: Static site-level observations of sand, silt, and clay content, including sampling depths and geographic coordinates where available.

CERN is a founding GERI partner but is not represented in the current ecological drought pilot release.

Additional GERI site-aligned datasets

The portal also provides three complementary datasets prepared for GERI sites through standalone workflows. These products are not outputs of the core GERI cross-infrastructure harmonization pipeline.

  • Aboveground net primary productivity (ANPP): Annual site-level ANPP estimates derived from the 500 m MODIS MOD17A3HGF Version 6.1 net primary productivity product. Total NPP was converted to ANPP using aboveground allocation fractions based on MODIS land-cover classifications, with source quality information and processing assumptions retained.
  • Evapotranspiration (ET): Monthly reference evapotranspiration estimates for the GERI sites from 1981–2024, calculated from NASA POWER reanalysis inputs using both the FAO-56 Penman–Monteith and Priestley–Taylor methods. The two products provide complementary estimates based on different representations of atmospheric and radiative controls.
  • Global gridded soil texture: Site-level topsoil sand, silt, and clay estimates extracted from OpenLandMap, SoilGrids 2.0, and the Harmonized World Soil Database v2.0. Source-specific values, depths, resolutions, and USDA texture classifications are retained to support comparison among products rather than being combined into a single estimate.

These datasets provide additional productivity, water-balance, and edaphic context for cross-site analyses while preserving the provenance and methodological distinctions of their underlying sources.

Quality, provenance, and responsible reuse

The harmonization process does not erase differences among the contributing source systems. Each dataset is accompanied by metadata and provenance describing its source, transformations, standardization decisions, and validation procedures.

The final time-series products include a harmonized quality_flag identifying observations that require review. Source-network quality information is evaluated internally during validation and incorporated into the harmonized flag where available.

Users should review the dataset-specific metadata, provenance, quality information, and source acknowledgements before analysis or redistribution.

Accessing the data

Datasets can be discovered and downloaded through the GERI DataONE portal and accessed programmatically through the DataONE R package. Processing code and technical documentation are maintained separately to support transparency and reproducibility. More information about the access and developer tools is available in the Support tab.
